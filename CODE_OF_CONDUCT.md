# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent to people. Argue about code, not about each other.

## Standards

Expected:

- Assume competence and good faith. The person you are replying to has
  probably thought about this.
- Criticise the work, specifically. "This breaks on a truncated file at offset
  0" is useful; "this is bad" is not.
- Accept that measurement beats opinion. If you claim something is faster,
  show the benchmark. If someone shows you a benchmark that contradicts you,
  update.
- Say "I was wrong" plainly when you were. It costs nothing and it is how a
  codebase gets correct.

Not acceptable:

- Personal attacks, insults, or dismissiveness about someone's experience level
- Harassment of any kind, public or private
- Sexualised language or imagery
- Publishing others' private information without permission
- Sustained disruption of technical discussion

## Scope

Applies in the issue tracker, pull requests, commit messages, and anywhere
someone is representing the project.

## Enforcement

Report by opening a private security advisory, or by contacting a
maintainer directly through GitHub. Reports stay confidential.

Maintainers may edit or remove contributions that violate this document, and
may ban repeat or serious offenders. Enforcement decisions are explained to
the person affected.

Maintainers who do not hold themselves to this are a worse problem than
contributors who do not, and should expect to be told so.
